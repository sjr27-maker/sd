A1.2 Mathematical Statements Revisited Recall, that a ‘statement’ is a meaningful sentence which is not an order, or an exclamation or a question. For example, ‘Which two teams are playing in the PROOFS IN MATHEMATICS 113 Cricket World Cup Final?’ is a question, not a statement. ‘Go and finish your homework’ is an order, not a statement. ‘What a fantastic goal!’ is an exclamation, not a statement. Remember, in general, statements can be one of the following:  always true  always false  ambiguous In Class IX, you have also studied that in mathematics, a statement is acceptable only if it is either always true or always false. So, ambiguous sentences are not considered as mathematical statements. Let us review our understanding with a few examples.
